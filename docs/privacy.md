## 1. Principles

- **Minimization.** We collect only what the platform cannot work without.
  - About a child: a pseudonym, an avatar from a set, the age category, the country, the interface language, the presentation style, the default solving mode, the hint mode and the adult's setting "ask before showing" the solution; the grade, if a module asks for it; interests, once the format gives a task styled to them (R98). There is no name, photo, date of birth, school or contact details; the photo of a page from paper, which may hold a name or a school, stays on the device, and goes to the AI only to find the page's tasks (section 6).
  - The adult's contact details: only those without which sign-in and the subscription do not work.
- **Pseudonymization, not anonymity.** In the system a learner is a UUID and a pseudonym. Under GDPR such data stays personal (recital 26), and under COPPA a persistent identifier is personal information too. So we do not promise "full anonymity" (as privacy 1 canvas-api).
- **Control lies with the account owner.** An adult learner manages their own data; for a child, a parent or guardian does: consent, viewing, export, deletion (R06, R09).
- **Privacy by default**, by the UK Age Appropriate Design Code: no public profiles, no communication between learners, no geolocation, no advertising and no profiling for marketing purposes.
- **Strokes stay on the device.** Strokes with their time and pressure are stored on the learner's device. What goes for checking is a picture or a crop of the work; the server holds it only in the memory of the request and does not store it (R09; as R42 canvas-api). While the storage of a profile's works is on, the server keeps the work as the device holds it, a stored work, which it does not read (section 6; R113).
- **The AI sees the work but does not know whose it is.** The platform passes the AI no name, no pseudonym, no identifiers and no data of the adult. But the picture may hold anything the learner wrote, a name included, and the photo of a page from paper whatever is on the page (section 6).
- **Honest retention periods.** Deletion is described with every place where data remains: backups, logs, other devices — they erase the data at their next request to `api` within an hour after the deletion, otherwise at the next sign-in on them, and a device that is not opened keeps it until the browser or the adult erases it (architecture 4.5) —, the sign-in service, the AI providers' logs and the record of consent (R117). We give no promise that "nothing will remain anywhere". Data on the device may also be erased by the browser itself, without any action of the account owner: when space runs short, when the site's data is cleared, and Safari erases "all of a website's script-writable storage after seven days of Safari use without user interaction on the site" ([WebKit, Full Third-Party Cookie Blocking and More](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/), 2020-03-24, read by `app` 2026-11-02).

## 3. Age

### 3.1. Age category

| Category | What applies (top level) |
|---|---|
| under 13 | COPPA (USA): verifiable parental consent before data is collected. GDPR Art. 8 and UK GDPR: parental consent. UK AADC |
| from 13 to the country's age of digital consent | GDPR Art. 8: parental consent. UK AADC. Where the age of consent is 13, as in the United Kingdom, this category is empty |
| from the age of digital consent to 18 | consent — the learner themselves. UK AADC — up to 18. The AI providers' terms on minors (section 5.3) |
| 18 and over | the laws on adults' data; in the EU, GDPR |

- **The age of digital consent** under GDPR is 16. A country may lower it, but not below 13; in the United Kingdom it is 13.
- **By country**, the age of digital consent is in the table, [the part `consent-age/`](../consent-age/README.md): each country of the EU and the EEA, the United Kingdom and the USA, with the law and its provision, a quote, the primary source, the date it was read and the day from which the age applies (R134). In the USA there is no federal age above 13: COPPA requires parental consent under 13, and the state laws are section 3.4 (R140).
- **A country outside the table** has 18: there consent for a learner under 18 is given by a parent, and the category "from the age of digital consent to 18" is empty. The platform does not know a law that nobody has read, and an error in this direction is a parent's consent for a teenager who could have given their own (R134).
- **Art. 8 GDPR** applies when the lawful basis of the processing is consent. The basis (R116): an adult's own data — contract, Art. 6(1)(b); the data of a learner under 18 — consent, Art. 6(1)(a): below the age of digital consent, the parent's, under Art. 8, and under 13 also verifiable under COPPA; above it, the learner's own.
- **So the country is needed:** without it one cannot determine which category, for example, a 14-year-old belongs to.

### 3.2. What depends on the age category

- who owns the account and gives consent;
- which AI routes and which providers are available (section 5);
- voice input (section 6); a task from paper, with the photo of its page, is open to every category, and which provider may serve its AI is the admission table's (section 5.1; R162);
- which modules are available: a module may mark content as unsuitable for a category (concepts 4);
- what the learner is shown: under 18 — at work with a rubric and in the section "Help", open from any screen, — where to turn if something is wrong: to an adult they trust, and to the helplines of their country (R127).

### 3.3. How age is given

- The account owner gives the learner's age and the country (R06).
- The platform computes the age category and stores it, the country, the date when the age was given, and the country's age of digital consent by which the category was given — from 13 to 18, a property of the law, not of the learner (R134). It stores neither the date of birth nor the age in years.
- The age in years reaches the server in the application for child consent and lives only in the memory of the request, and for the owner profile the category is `adult` by the owner's own statement, without an age (R98).
- Once a year the category is updated: `app` asks the adult to give the age again when the profile is flagged — a year after the age was given. Until the update the previous category applies: it is stricter, because the learner has only grown older since.
- When the country changes, the age is given again: the age in years is not stored, and there is nothing to recompute the category from.
- Likewise when the country's age of digital consent has become higher than the one by which the category was given: a learner who gave consent themselves may have ended up below the new age, and before the next work the age is given again — also when the table recorded later a law passed earlier. A lowered age waits for the yearly update: the previous category is stricter (R134; [the part `consent-age/`](../consent-age/README.md), section 6).
- In all three cases the adult gives the age and the country by one method, `RestateAge`, and a new category that needs a different consent waits for it: the learner's own consent or a parent's consent with a new consent request code (R136).
- The account owner confirms being an adult by the statement "I am 18" with its date, and for a child's profile the consent method confirms it too (R116; R118).
- **The presentation style** is not a legal concept: the length of phrases, voicing, the persona, ranks. It can be chosen separately (concepts 13).

### 4.1. Retention periods

The periods by kind of data that the written retention policy gathers (R128); the fields of each store are architecture 3.2 api and architecture 3.2 app, and the periods of the consent record, the scan of the form, the samples and their forms, a problem report, the mail to the operator and a stored work are in the table of section 4 (R113, R116, R117, R123, R125, R126, R138).

- **The device** (R04 app). A task's work with the contexts of its submissions lives until the end of the attempt, and an unfinished one 30 days without changes. An essay is an artifact: it is kept until an adult erases it. A lesson's open view and the state of a demo — until the lesson is passed or 30 days without changes. The cache of open views by package digest — 30 days without access, and a withdrawn version goes at once. The history of tasks from paper — the photo of each page, its frames, the crops and the work — is not bound by the end of the attempt or its 30 days: it lives until the profile is erased, or the owner signs out on the device, as below, and the profile's export holds it (R162). Deleting a profile erases its database on the device, and the account owner's sign-out — everything on the device that belongs to the account. The browser may erase data earlier by itself (section 1).
- **The database of `api`** (R05 api).
  - Attempts, lesson runs, and submissions of tasks with a rubric with the marks of the criteria, live while the profile lives (R13 and R15 api; R87). An open attempt without actions for 7 days is closed "abandoned" (R10 api), and an open run without actions for 30 days ends "abandoned", and the row stays (R13 api).
  - The words of hints live until the attempt is closed, so no longer than 8 days after the last action, and after that what remains of a hint is the rung's number and the source — the model or the fallback text.
  - The records of idempotency keys, without content, — no longer than 2 days (R17 api); connected modules and engine choices — while the profile lives.
  - The counters of the platform AI quota, the profile's and the account's (R12 api). A counter is created with the first request to platform AI in a family day, and is needed for its own day and for one more day after its end — for requests across midnight. So it lives no longer than 2 days after the end of its day, and leaves Neon's history one day later. Deleting a profile takes its counters with it, and the account's counters stay until the account is deleted: otherwise a deletion and a new profile would lift the spending cap.
  - An account from which no request with a token came for 12 months is deleted with its profiles and its user at the sign-in service, and of the consents remains the minimal record of R117.
  - Of an attempt of a task from paper, `api` keeps its key, its verdicts, the count of wrong answers and the words of its hints only while it is open, so no longer than 8 days after the last action, and nothing once it ends: its history is the device's. Never the photo, a crop or the work (R162). A problem report about its tutor's words stays as its own row of section 4 says (R123). Where `api` holds them is the contracts' (RUN.md, T225).
  - OpenFGA's `changelog` — no longer than two days: OpenFGA does not clean it itself, and a daily job deletes the records older than a day, as R27 canvas-api.
- **The sign-in service**, Firebase Auth (R03 api), sets its periods itself. It keeps the IP addresses of its logs for a few weeks — "Firebase Authentication keeps logged IP addresses for a few weeks" —, and the rest about the user until `api` deletes the user, and up to 180 days after that: "It retains other authentication information until the Firebase customer initiates deletion of the associated user, after which data is removed from live and backup systems within 180 days" ([Privacy and Security in Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy), updated 2026-10-29, read 2026-11-02; sources 4.4). A user of the sign-in service without an account of `api` — one who signed in without an invite code, or again after a deletion, with a new UID — is deleted by an operator's job after 30 days without a sign-in or a token refresh, and then come the same 180 days of Firebase (R21 api): a year would keep the Google account of a person whom the platform never admitted (section 1).
- **Hosting and logs** (R06 and R07 infra; sources 4.3, 4.4; read 2026-11-08). Firebase Hosting keeps the IP addresses of requests itself, globally, and the period cannot be configured: "Hosting retains IP data for a few months" ([Privacy and Security in Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy), "Last modified: September 24, 2026"). The platform's logs — 30 days in a bucket in `us-east4`. Cloud Run's request logs Google receives and discards before storage: "Exclusion filters are applied after log entries are received by the Logging API or Telemetry API" ([Route log entries](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/routing/overview)). When the samples page moves to Hosting, its notice and form name this period (R128). Traces — section 4, the row "Logs".
- **Backups** — Neon's history window: what is deleted lives in the history until it leaves the window. Neon's default and largest windows are "Free 6 hours 6 hours (capped at 1 GB of history) Launch 1 day 7 days Scale 1 day 30 days" ([History window](https://neon.com/docs/postgres/backup-restore/history-window), the page has no date, read 2026-11-02; sources 4.5). The deployment of `api` is the Launch plan with a window of 1 day (R05 api), so what is deleted leaves the history within a day.
- **The audit** (R07 infra; sources 4.3; read 2026-11-08). The Admin Activity audit — in `_Required`, 400 days, in `global`: "you can't configure, exclude, or disable them" ([Cloud Audit Logs overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/docs/audit)), "_Required … 400 days Not configurable" ([Quotas and limits](https://docs.cloud.google.com/logging/quotas)), and a record holds the caller's email and `callerIp`. The callers are the operator and CI. `SignUp` of Identity Platform — the creation of a user — writes Admin Activity too, while the Google sign-in, `SignInWithIdp`, gives no audit logs ([Identity Platform audit logging](https://docs.cloud.google.com/identity-platform/docs/audit-logging)); nothing in the project `novatrail` calls `SignUp`, and the audit holds no entry of an account owner or a learner (R128).

## 5. AI routes

### 5.1. Platform AI

- The platform calls the AI under its own contracts with the providers.
- **Requirements for a provider:**
  - the provider does not train models on learners' data;
  - zero data retention is mandatory where the provider's terms require it: at OpenAI, for the personal data of children under 13 or under the age of digital consent (section 5.3). For a minor in other cases, minimal retention: zero data retention or an exclusion of content from the abuse logs, approved by the provider, a deployment fact of each cell of the platform AI under 18 (R96; R121). An adult's work goes under the provider's usual retention — Anthropic deletes it within 30 days, OpenAI keeps its abuse monitoring logs up to 30 days, either longer by law or for the provider's safety (sources 2.9) —, which the privacy policy names: the cell of `adult` needs no `minimal_retention` (R96; R121; R149);
  - a processor agreement (DPA): at OpenAI and Anthropic it is part of their terms by itself, and at OpenAI the author also signs a copy (R121);
  - only the routes that zero data retention covers: at OpenAI, Responses with `store: false`, without `/v1/files`; at Anthropic, Messages, without the Files API and Batch; the Anthropic models that need retention of 30 days — Fable 5, 5.1 and 5.2, Mythos 5, 5.1 and 5.2 (sources 2.9) — are outside the list of those admitted for learners' works (R121);
  - the data region is the USA, one place for all learners (R109), and it is not a deployment fact (R96);
  - for minors, terms that expressly permit them, and the measures they require: their cells are opened also by a fact of measures, which the operator declares only when the provider's whole list of measures is met and the author has decided by analysis that they are sufficient (R96, R112, R123).
- **What goes to the AI:** the work (a picture, a crop, text, sound); the task's data — the task statement, and for the analysis also the key and the task's solution (R55), for the correct answer words the key, the solution and the task's techniques (concepts 10), for assessment by a rubric its criteria; for assessment by a rubric what goes is the text read from the essay, without the picture (R75); the observations needed for the words; of the presentation style, only what changes the words, and voicing does not go (section 4). Identifiers, the pseudonym and the adult's data never go.
- **A task from paper** (R162; section 6): the photo of the page, in one request, to find the tasks; a picked task's crop, to read its statement into a formula, for the solver, and where it is no formula or the solver cannot take it, to one solution, for the AI's key — so in up to two calls, at the attempt's first check, by the route of the attempt, as its reading goes ([the contracts text](../contracts/README.md), sections 15 and 16); the work, to one reader at a time; each call to up to three models of one provider in turn, in one request, the next only when the one before did not do (R164); and to the analysis and the hint what `tutor` decides (RUN.md, T230). Its three capabilities, `task_finding`, `statement_reading` and `task_solving`, are rules of the admission table, open to every category, and take the same cells and facts as any other: an adult's page goes under the provider's usual retention, as an adult's work does, and a minor's only where `minimal_retention` holds (R96; R121; R149).
- Models only after eval (R07).
- **Admission** is by the admission table of `spec`, [`admission/`](../admission/README.md): to which provider, on which AI route, the work of a learner of which age category may be given, and under which deployment facts. The admission function of `tutor` reads it before an attempt and before each call of a model with a learner's work (R96; R57 tutor).

### 5.2. Own key

- A learner or an account owner connects their own key of an AI provider.
- The calls go in the name of the key's owner: the provider sees their billing account and ties each request to it. The provider's terms are between the provider and the key's owner.
- The key is the key owner's secret. It lies on the device only if the key's owner so decided on this device, and otherwise only in the tab's memory while their profile is open; on the server only in the memory of the request, not in the database and not in the logs. There is no encryption in the browser: a key saved on the device can be taken by whoever holds the device, and `app` says so plainly. The key is not in the export (R84; R08 app).
- The key goes with each request of the owner profile that calls the AI, and with a request of a profile whose category is not `adult`, `api` does not accept it (R04 api; О-4).
- The providers' terms, by the author's decision, allow giving the key to `api` (R84). At OpenAI the contract is the Services Agreement, and in it §3.1 and §3.3(g) are on access to the account and on sharing keys; at Anthropic keys go under the Commercial Terms, which have no provision on sharing a key, and the help center describes a key in third-party tools (sources 2.3).
- The platform applies its own rules here too: what goes into a call — as in section 5.1; which models are admitted; to which age categories the route is open.
- The own key is closed for minors (R122; О-4).

- **The Gemini API** in its AI Studio variant does not suit a platform open to children. The condition concerns the service as a whole, so the route is closed for all learners, adults too — both through the platform AI and with an own key.

## 6. Voice, photos and artifacts

- **The tutor's voicing** is for everyone: it is output, not data collection, as long as the text does not leave the device.
  - So `app` names the voice itself, only a speech synthesis voice of the device whose `localService` is true and whose name has no "Online" (Т-13; R07 app). Remote synthesis would receive the tutor's words bypassing `api`, without a contract with the platform, and feedback by a rubric quotes the learner's words (R70).
  - `localService` is known to the browser, not to the system. On Android and Linux, Chrome gives the page every system voice as local — `localService` is true for a network one too (R118) —, so whether the system synthesizes on the device the page cannot learn, and there voicing is turned on by the adult, having read why (sources 5.4). The browser gives the text to the synthesis engine by the adult's choice, as to their screen reader; this does not count as a disclosure by the platform, by the author's decision on Claude's analysis (R112; R118).
- **Voice input.**
  - Under COPPA a voiceprint is a biometric identifier, that is, personal information (16 CFR 312.2, "voiceprints"; sources 3.1).
  - The exception from parental consent is audio with a child's voice without other personal data. It must be collected only to respond to a specific request of the child, used for nothing else, disclosed to no one, and deleted immediately after the response (16 CFR 312.5(c)(9); sources 3.1).
  - Whether pronunciation assessment and passing the audio to an AI provider fall under the exception the author decides by analysis (R112; П-5). Until the decision, voice input is for adults only (О-6).
- **Photos from paper** — a task from the learner's own page (R162).
  - A page may hold a name — a worksheet's field "Name" —, a school, a teacher's notes, other people's names and texts: more than the task, and more than the learner wrote.
  - The whole page goes to the AI in one request, only to find the tasks: from the device through `api` to `tutor`, in the memory of the request, to up to three models of one provider in turn, in one request, the next only when the one before did not do (R164), and only the task frames come back. The answer holds no text of the page, and the server keeps none of it (R09).
  - At the provider the page is a work of the learner's category: an adult's goes under the provider's usual retention, and a minor's only where minimal retention holds — zero data retention or the exclusion of content from the abuse monitoring logs (section 5.1; R121).
  - Before the camera opens, `app` reminds the learner to keep names out of the photo, as before any work that goes to the AI whole (R127).
  - `app` sends and keeps the picture re-encoded, without the metadata of its file — where it was taken, the device, the time —: the platform has no geolocation (section 1).
  - `app` cuts each picked task's crop on the device by its frame. The crop goes for the key — to read its statement into a formula, and where it is no formula or the solver cannot take it, to one solution —, and the crop with the learner's strokes on it, with the sheet's strokes, is the work for the check (concepts 8); each, as the page, to up to three models of one provider in turn, in one request, the next only when the one before did not do (R164).
  - The photo, the frames, the crops and the work stay on the device, in the profile's storage: the profile's history and its export hold them, and they are erased with the profile. They are no stored work on the server, even while the storage of works is on (R113), and they go into no shared pool (R162).
  - The measure is enough, by the author's decision of 2026-11-18 (RUN.md, T223): the reminder does not stop a name, but the page goes in one request, to a later model of the same provider only when the one before found no task (R164), and a name goes further only where it lies inside a task's frame, with that crop and its work; each to a processor alone, tied to no identifier, and a minor's only where minimal retention holds; it is risk 2 of the DPIA, medium, which the author accepted (dpia 6 and 7).
- **Artifacts** — drawings, essays, recordings — belong to the learner. On the server, any work of the profile — the handwriting on a task with a key too, as `storage-consent` says, "each work of this profile" — is kept as a stored work, as the device holds it; a recording is not, nor a task from paper (R162). Works are stored on the server only if the account owner turned this on for the profile, and a learner who gives consent themselves, for themselves, in their own profile; by default, no (R113). The switch says that an essay may hold personal matters, about health and the family among them, and that nobody reads it: turning it on is explicit consent to this (R127). Its words are the text `storage-consent` of the part `legal/` (R127).
- **An essay** is personal data of the learner and of those the learner names in it (R127). Before submitting a work that goes to the AI whole, the platform reminds the learner not to write names. It does not look for signs of harm in the essay, and to a learner under 18 it always shows where to turn if something is wrong.
