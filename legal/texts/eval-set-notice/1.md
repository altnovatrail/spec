# Eval set: what happens to your samples

You are about to write samples for the eval set of a module: published samples by which every new AI model is tested before learners get it. Before your first sample you have signed the volunteer's form with the organizer of the eval set; this page says what that form says.

## Who we are

The samples page is run by NovaTrail's operator, ALT EdTech LLC, a limited liability company organized in Texas, in the United States: the controller of your samples and your form. The organizer acts for it, under its instructions. You can reach it by post: ALT EdTech LLC, 12828 Willow Centre Dr, Ste D, Houston, TX 77066-3043, USA; by telephone: +1 224 933 2890; by email: `altedtech.info@gmail.com`. That email is Gmail, which Google runs under its own terms, not as our processor.

The organizer takes no form from a person who lives in the European Union, the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom until our privacy policy names our representatives there.

## What a sample holds

- the final shapes of what you wrote — no history of your writing, no pressure, no time — and the crops of your answers;
- the markup: what each answer says, and for an essay its text and its marks against the module's criteria — two people mark it, each on their own, and where they differ, a third decides;
- the license, Apache-2.0.

The sample holds no writer code, no name and no signature, and you write none on it. Write on made-up topics, with no names of real people. Before a sample goes anywhere, the organizer looks for a name on it by hand, and a sample with a name, or any that fails the check, is erased, and you write it again.

## Published for ever

Your samples are published with the module, in its public repository, under the Apache-2.0 license. Once published, a version of the module keeps them for ever, and copies are out of anyone's reach. In the module's tests they run at the AI providers whose models are measured, Anthropic and OpenAI. NovaTrail uses them for evaluating AI and trains no model of its own on them — that would need a new form; once published under the Apache-2.0 license, anyone may use them as that license allows, training a model among them.

You can withdraw at any time by giving the organizer, or ALT EdTech LLC at the email or the address above, your name and the date of your form. Until a sample is added to the module's repository, it is then erased within 30 days. Once it is added, the organizer takes it out of the module's next version and its tests within 30 days; the versions already published, and the copies others made under the Apache-2.0 license, stay. Withdrawing does not make unlawful what was done before it.

Your form, with your name and signature, and the organizer's record of which samples were given under it, lie in the operator's closed storage, at Google Cloud in Virginia, United States, apart from the repository, as long as any of your samples is in the module's repository or in a published version of it, as the evidence of their license; a form under which no sample was added is erased 12 months after you signed it.

## Where it goes until then

You save the file on your device and send it, with the scan of your form, to the organizer by the upload link they give you, which works for one day. If Safari saves your downloads to iCloud Drive, the file is in Apple's cloud too until you erase it; once the organizer has it, erase it from your device. Until it is published, it lies in the operator's closed storage, at Google Cloud in Virginia, United States; the organizer reads it there or on their own device and puts no copy anywhere else.

## Why we may

We process your samples and your form with your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a)), and keep the form while your samples are published to show the license you gave them (Articles 7(1) and 17(3)(e)). NovaTrail's privacy policy, in its sections "The samples page" and "Your rights", says the rest: your rights to see, correct and erase your data, and to complain to a data protection authority.
