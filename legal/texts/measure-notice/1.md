# Measurement: what happens to your samples

You are about to write samples for a measurement: they test how well AI reads handwriting, and nothing else. Before your first sample you have signed the form of this measurement with its organizer; this page says what that form says.

## What a sample holds

- what you wrote, as strokes: their final shapes and also the history of your writing — the time and the course of the pen, stroke by stroke; it holds no pressure. We treat that history as biometric data, and you give your explicit consent to it on the form;
- the crops of your answers, and their markup: what each answer says;
- your writer code, which the page makes and does not remember. It links your samples to each other, never to your name. Keep it: you write it on your form, and you name it to withdraw.

The sample holds no name, and you write none on it: before a sample goes anywhere, the organizer looks for a name on it by hand, and a sample with a name is erased, and you write it again.

## Who sees it

- the organizer of the measurement;
- the runner that measures the reading, and the AI provider whose models it measures: Anthropic.

Nobody compares writers by their handwriting, and no one uses your writing to tell who you are. Your samples are never published.

## Where it is kept

You save the file on your device and send it to the organizer by the upload link they give you, which works for one day. If Safari saves your downloads to iCloud Drive, the file is in Apple's cloud too until you erase it; once the organizer has it, erase it from your device. The file and your form lie in the operator's closed storage, at Google Cloud in Virginia, United States; the organizer reads them there or on their own device and puts no copy anywhere else.

## How long

- 2 years from the session you wrote it in, and then it is erased. Your form stays 5 years after all the samples under it are erased, as the evidence of your consent.
- You can withdraw by naming your writer code: within 30 days the organizer erases your samples, their copies, and the reports of the runner that name them. What stays is what the AI provider keeps under its terms, and any copy on your own device, which you erase yourself. Conclusions written without your samples' IDs, such as a measured threshold, stay.
