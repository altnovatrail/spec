# For you, the learner

This says what NovaTrail does with your work and your data, in short. The whole of it is in our privacy policy, which the adult of your account accepted; you can read it in the app. NovaTrail is run by a company, ALT EdTech LLC, in Texas, in the United States.

## Your work

- What you write stays on your device. To be checked, a picture or a crop of it, or the text read from an essay, goes to an AI provider, OpenAI or Anthropic, without your name or any ID, and our server does not keep it, unless keeping your works there is switched on.
- The picture shows whatever you wrote, so do not write your name or the real names of people you know.
- A program decides whether an answer is right. The tutor is an AI, not a person.

## What we keep

- a pseudonym, an avatar, your age category and country, and the settings of your profile — never your name, your date of birth, your school or where you are;
- what you have done and shown you can do, so that the next task suits you.

The adult of the account can export it, and delete it.

## Who sees it

- The adult who owns the account can see your progress: your attempts, how your writing was marked, and the mistakes you make again and again.
- No other person sees your progress. If the adult reports a problem with the tutor's words at a reply, the operator reads that report.
- We pass data on only where the law requires it or a life is at risk. No one buys your data or advertises with it.

## Who decides

Until you reach the age of digital consent of your country, the adult of the account decides about your data. Once a year, and when your country changes or raises that age, the adult gives your age again; when you have reached it, you are asked in your profile to confirm or withdraw that consent, and then you decide. When the age given again shows that you are 18, the app tells you that the protections the law gives a minor no longer apply to you.

## If something is wrong

If the tutor says something wrong or hurtful, tell the adult of your account: they can report it at that reply. Tell an adult you trust, or call a helpline of your country: the app's "Help" names them. If you or someone else is in danger now, call your country's emergency number.
